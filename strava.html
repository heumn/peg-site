The Strava flow.

What actually happens when a ride leaves Peg — and what every button on a finished ride does. No magic, a few hand-offs.

Upload, step by step

1

You tap Upload to Strava.

The orange capsule at the foot of any finished ride. It never remembers state — it uploads, every time you ask.

2

The first time, Strava asks.

Consent opens in the Strava app itself (or a sign-in sheet if the app isn't installed). One Authorize, and you bounce straight back into Peg. This happens once — after that, uploads go directly.

3

Peg writes the ride file.

A .fit with every second of power, cadence and heart rate, one lap per workout section, and the device name "Peg iOS app". The title is your workout's name; the description is two lines — structure and outcome, then the numbers.

4

Strava processes it.

The button shows a spinner while Strava chews (usually a few seconds). Uploading the same ride twice gets Strava's own duplicate answer back — Peg doesn't second-guess it.

5

Checkmark, then hand-off.

The capsule flips to ✓ Uploaded for a moment, and the Strava app opens on the fresh activity. This jump is deliberate — your ride is now over there, so Peg takes you there.

Every button on a finished ride

FOOT OF THE RIDE

Upload to Strava

Uploads this ride's file with the pre-written title and description. States: idle → spinner ("Uploading to Strava") → ✓ Uploaded → the Strava app opens on the activity. Errors surface as an alert with Strava's own words. It asks for nothing else — no photos, no location.

Ride again

Starts a new session of the exact workout this ride was — the snapshot from that day, even if you've edited the workout since. If a ride is already running, it re-opens that one instead of starting a second.

SHARE MENU · TOP RIGHT

Share image

Renders the ride as the 1080 × 1350 poster and opens the system share sheet from the bottom — AirDrop, Messages, Instagram, Save Image. Nothing touches your photo library unless you choose Save Image yourself.

Export ride

The raw .fit file, through the same share sheet. It's the same file Strava gets — TrainingPeaks, intervals.icu and Garmin Connect all ingest it. Named like Peg 2026-07-25 Rønnestad 30-15.fit.

Save card for Strava

Saves the poster to your photo library, then opens Strava — on the ride you uploaded this session, otherwise just the app. Attaching is then Edit → Add Photo → first image. This is the only button that ever asks for Photos access, and only the "add" kind — Peg can put images in, never look at them.

POSTER SCREEN · AFTER YOU END A RIDE

Done  Share

Done closes the ride — it's already saved to History either way. Share is the same image and .fit pair as the share menu, for when you want the poster out in the world before you've left the bike.

Why the image can't attach itself.

Strava's public API accepts ride files, titles and descriptions — but not photos. Photo attachment is reserved for their partner apps. So Peg does the next honest thing: the upload carries your data and the device name, and the poster travels by your hand — the share sheet for anywhere, or Save card for Strava plus one Edit → Add Photo for the activity itself. If Strava ever opens that door, the card will walk through it by itself.
